# Week 12 — Troubleshooting Narrative

1. **What went wrong or could realistically have gone wrong?** The `CVNP1606-Lab` VM began restarting and reached Automatic Repair, even after restoring the assigned baseline snapshot. Separately, the new `ACME-W12-ScreenLock` profile initially showed Pending on the pilot device instead of a successful result.
2. **What evidence did you check first?** I inspected the Automatic Repair screen, the `W01_CleanBaseline` snapshot, the policy setting and assignment screens, and the first Intune assignment-status report. I also checked `1606MIDTERM`'s device overview, where the last check-in was November 8.
3. **What did you try?** I restored `W01_CleanBaseline`, used the Windows host browser to finish policy creation, and then investigated the separate `1606Midterm` device. The main lab VM was updated after it restarted repeatedly; I also started the midterm VM and synced its work/school connection to Intune.
4. **What fixed it, or what would you try next?** Updating Windows appeared to stabilize `CVNP1606-Lab` long enough to continue. For the pending Intune policy, bringing the actual targeted VM (`1606Midterm`) online and syncing its work account resolved the stale device check-in. Any further device compliance failures require separate investigation.
5. **How did you verify the result?** Intune showed `1606MIDTERM` checked in on **November 15, 2026, at 4:15 PM**. Under **Devices → All devices → 1606MIDTERM → Device configuration**, `ACME-W12-ScreenLock` showed a green **Succeeded** result. I captured this screenshot as evidence.
6. **What was the support or deployment impact?** The policy was verified as applied on a pilot device instead of being left unconfirmed. Other technicians can follow the runbook and check actual Intune statuses. The VM still showed **Not compliant** overall, and a full Autopilot/ESP production rollout needs separate verification before new-hire devices ship.
